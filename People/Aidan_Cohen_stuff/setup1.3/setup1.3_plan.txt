GROUP TYPES 

**G1 — Category & local discovery (no brand named)**  
**Benchmarks:** When the user asks AI for a **type of business in a place** and has **not** picked your client or a competitor yet — who gets recommended?  
**Real behavior:** “Best / top / near me / in [area]” for the category. This is **unbranded demand**; AI’s shortlist is the game.  
**Examples:** “best [service] in [city]”, “[category] near [landmark]”.  
**Not:** Your client or a competitor as the main target (those are G2/G3).

**LLM prompt (template bank):**  
Generate **~500** unique query **templates** (one per line) for **G1**.  
**Intent & volume:** Favor phrasing people actually search in high volume and that imply **choosing a provider soon** (ready to pick / visit / call / book), not idle trivia. Prefer **commercial + local** patterns (best/top/near me/open now/reviews implied/cheap vs luxury tradeoffs) where still unbranded.  
**Variance:** Mix **many structures** — questions, commands, “I need…”, “looking for…”, comparisons of approach, urgency, distance, time windows, constraints — and **many ideas** (open now, walk-in, same day, quality vs price, fastest, most trusted *as a category*, neighborhoods, landmarks, driving distance, “good enough”, “worth it for tourists”, etc.). Avoid repeating the same skeleton; vary length and angle.  
**Placeholders:** Use **only** placeholders from **Complete list of value bank types** below. For G1 **do not** use `[client]`, `[competitor]`, `[competitor_a]`, `[competitor_b]`, `[option_a]`, `[option_b]`. Use the **rest** of the list liberally and in **many combinations** across the 500 lines.  
**Output:** Plain text, one template per line, no numbering.

---

**G2 — Direct brand (your client named)**  
**Benchmarks:** **Navigational + branded** queries — the user already has your client in mind and asks AI for **facts, fit, or next step** about **that** business.  
**Real behavior:** Hours, services, “does [Client]…”, location, policies — **high intent** once they’re yours to lose.  
**Examples:** “[Client] pricing”, “does [Client] do X”, “[Client] hours [city]”.  
**Not:** Competitor-only queries (G3).

**LLM prompt (template bank):**  
Generate **~500** unique query **templates** (one per line) for **G2**.  
**Intent & volume:** Prioritize **high-intent branded** patterns (pricing, hours, book, locations, services, policies, “do they offer…”, insurance, cancellation, wait times, packages) that reflect **real search volume** and **near-purchase** decisions.  
**Variance:** Many structures (direct questions, implied task, troubleshooting, “is [client] good for…”, first-timer vs repeat, mobile vs desktop-style phrasing). Many ideas (specific SKUs/services, edge cases, same-day, membership, gift cards, corporate, refunds).  
**Placeholders:** From the master list below, **`[client]` is required in every line.** Do **not** use competitor placeholders as the only brand (that’s G3). Combine `[client]` with `[city]`, `[service]`, `[product]`, `[goal]`, `[timeframe]`, `[price_or_budget]`, `[qualifier]`, `[zip_or_area]`, etc., across the set.  
**Output:** Plain text, one template per line, no numbering.

---

**G3 — Competitors & alternatives (switching / competitive set)**       
**Benchmarks:** **Share of voice when the buyer is shopping the field** — named competitors, “alternatives to”, “vs”, leaving one provider for another.  
**Real behavior:** **Pre-purchase** or **switch**; AI often compares options in one reply.  
**Examples:** “alternatives to [Competitor]”, “[A] vs [B] in [city]” when brands are the anchors.  
**Not:** Generic “best X in city” with no named competitor (G1).

**LLM prompt (template bank):**  
Generate **~500** unique query **templates** (one per line) for **G3**.  
**Intent & volume:** Emphasize **switching**, **evaluation before purchase**, and **comparison** — phrasing that matches **high-volume competitive** search (alternatives, vs, cheaper than, better than, problems with X, leaving X).  
**Variance:** Many structures (vs, alternatives, “should I switch”, “if I liked X will I like Y”, multi-brand, “who beats…”, complaints-led). Many ideas (price, quality, speed, location, niche use cases).  
**Placeholders:** From the master list below, every line must include at least one of **`[competitor]`**, **`[competitor_a]`**, **`[competitor_b]`** (combine as needed). Do **not** use **only** `[client]` without a competitive frame. Mix in `[city]`, `[category]`, `[service]`, `[goal]`, `[price_or_budget]`, `[timeframe]`, `[qualifier]`, `[region]`, etc.  
**Output:** Plain text, one template per line, no numbering.

---

**G4 — Transactional & bottom-funnel (money, book, buy, now)**  
**Benchmarks:** Queries where **purchase or commitment** is explicit — **closest to converting**: price, cost, book, appointment, availability, deals, “how much”, “today / asap”.  
**Real behavior:** What people ask AI right before **paying or booking**; answers that omit or misstate you here hurt revenue.  
**Examples:** “how much does [thing] cost”, “book [service] same day [city]”, “cheap [category] under $X”.  
**Not:** Pure “is it good” without price/booking focus (G5).

**LLM prompt (template bank):**  
Generate **~500** unique query **templates** (one per line) for **G4**.  
**Intent & volume:** Maximize **purchase / booking / money** signals — the kinds of queries that keyword tools show as **high commercial intent** and strong volume (cost, quote, book, appointment, deal, insurance accepted, deposit, same day, walk-in, package price).  
**Variance:** Many structures (how much, total cost, hidden fees, payment plans, book tonight, waitlist, cancellation cost, “under $X”, compare quotes). Many ideas (urgency, insurance, tipping, add-ons, group booking, peak pricing).  
**Placeholders:** From the master list below; lean on `[price_or_budget]`, `[timeframe]`, `[city]`, `[neighborhood]`, `[service]`, `[category]`, `[product]`, `[goal]`, `[qualifier]`, `[client]`, `[competitor]` where it still reads **transaction-first** (not review-first).  
**Output:** Plain text, one template per line, no numbering.

---

**G5 — Trust, reviews & risk (pre-purchase anxiety)**  
**Benchmarks:** **De-risking** before spend — reviews, ratings, legit vs scam, worth it, “should I”, complaints. AI often **summarizes reputation** in one shot.  
**Real behavior:** **High stakes before buying**; bad or missing signal here loses the sale even if discovery was strong.  
**Examples:** “[Client] reviews”, “is [X] legit”, “[category] worth the money”.  
**Not:** Straight price shopping (G4).

**LLM prompt (template bank):**  
Generate **~500** unique query **templates** (one per line) for **G5**.  
**Intent & volume:** Focus on **pre-purchase trust** queries that still have **high search volume** (reviews, BBB, lawsuits, reddit, “too good to be true”, refund reputation). These should support a **buy decision**, not academic research.  
**Variance:** Many structures (is it legit, worth it, overrated, red flags, “anyone had issues”, comparison to expectations). Many ideas (quality consistency, safety, hygiene, upsell fear, warranty, authenticity).  
**Placeholders:** From the master list below; rotate `[client]`, `[competitor]`, `[category]`, `[service]`, `[city]`, `[neighborhood]`, `[goal]`, `[qualifier]`. Do **not** make **price quote** or **booking slot** the main hook (that’s G4).  
**Output:** Plain text, one template per line, no numbering.

---

**G6 — Fit: persona, occasion, constraint**  
**Benchmarks:** **Recommendation queries** where the user frames **who it’s for or the situation** — how AI **matches** businesses to a life context (often before shortlisting).  
**Real behavior:** “Best for families / date night / beginners / luxury…” — common in **conversational AI**; tests whether the client is **included in tailored answers**.  
**Examples:** “romantic [thing] in [area]”, “[service] for beginners near [Y]”.  
**Not:** No persona/occasion angle → G1.

**LLM prompt (template bank):**  
Generate **~500** unique query **templates** (one per line) for **G6**.  
**Intent & volume:** Bias toward **high-intent fit** queries (someone is **close to picking** but needs the right match): families, dates, accessibility, skill level, budget band, vibe, corporate, events, anxiety/special needs. Use phrasing that mirrors **high-volume long-tail** “best for X” patterns.  
**Variance:** Many structures (“for my mom”, “kid-friendly”, “if I’m picky”, “if I’m on a budget”, “anniversary”, “team building”). Many persona/occasion dimensions; avoid repeating one template shape.  
**Placeholders:** From the master list below; **`[persona_or_occasion]`** (and/or `[audience]`, `[use_case]`) must be **meaningfully used** in most lines. Also mix `[category]`, `[service]`, `[city]`, `[neighborhood]`, `[landmark]`, `[goal]`, `[qualifier]`, `[price_or_budget]`, `[timeframe]`.  
**Output:** Plain text, one template per line, no numbering.

---

**G7 — Head-to-head choice (shortlist / pick one)**  
**Benchmarks:** **Explicit decision** — “which one”, “A or B”, “best between” — user is **narrowing to a choice**; AI’s ranking and reasoning matter.  
**Real behavior:** **Late funnel**; overlaps purchase intent with G3/G4 but **structure** is “compare and pick”, not “list alternatives” alone.  
**Examples:** “which [category] is better in [place]”, “X or Y for [goal]”.  
**Not:** “Alternatives to X” without a tight pick-one frame (often G3).

**LLM prompt (template bank):**  
Generate **~500** unique query **templates** (one per line) for **G7**.  
**Intent & volume:** Force **decision** language that people use when **ready to choose** (which is better, pick one, final decision, “if you had to choose”). Favor patterns associated with **strong commercial follow-through**.  
**Variance:** Many structures (A vs B, ranked choice, “tie-breaker”, “if budget matters”, “if quality matters”, triage questions). Many ideas (brand vs brand, approach vs approach, location tradeoffs, speed vs quality).  
**Placeholders:** From the master list below; most lines should use **`[option_a]`** and **`[option_b]`** and/or **`[competitor_a]`** / **`[competitor_b]`** in a **forced-choice** frame. Add `[goal]`, `[city]`, `[category]`, `[service]`, `[price_or_budget]`, `[qualifier]`, `[timeframe]` for variety. **No** broad “list all alternatives” without a tight pick-one structure.  
**Output:** Plain text, one template per line, no numbering.

Complete list of value bank types (all placeholders — use only these in template banks)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Use only these bracketed tokens as variables. Do not invent new [tags]. When generating a bank, rotate and combine them so every type that the group allows shows up across the ~500 lines (heavy variety, not one template with every slot stuffed in).

`[client]` — business being benchmarked (your client)  
`[competitor]` — one named competitor  
`[competitor_a]` / `[competitor_b]` — two named entities in a comparison  
`[option_a]` / `[option_b]` — two choices in a forced pick (brand, product, or approach)  
`[category]` — business category / industry bucket  
`[service]` — service line or offering  
`[product]` — product or SKU-style item  
`[city]` — city  
`[neighborhood]` — neighborhood / district  
`[landmark]` — anchor place (“near the mall”, “by the stadium”)  
`[region]` — metro / county / wider area  
`[state_or_country]` — state or country  
`[zip_or_area]` — ZIP or informal area (“90210”, “midtown”)  
`[goal]` — outcome the user wants  
`[use_case]` — situation / job-to-be-done  
`[persona_or_occasion]` — who/when (date night, kids, wedding, beginner, etc.)  
`[audience]` — who it’s for if distinct from persona (seniors, students, tourists)  
`[price_or_budget]` — budget band, “under $X”, cheap vs luxury  
`[timeframe]` — today, tonight, this weekend, same day, walk-in  
`[qualifier]` — fast, 24/7, female-owned, award-winning, etc.

Rule: template banks may only use tokens from this list. Each group prompt above says what is required or forbidden for that group.
